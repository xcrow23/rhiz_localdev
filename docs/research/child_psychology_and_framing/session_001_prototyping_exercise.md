# Session 001: Prototyping Exercise with 9-Year-Old Collaborator

**Date**: 2026-01-10
**Participant**: Captain's daughter (age 9)
**Exercise**: "One thing that doesn't exist but maps to existing objects/structures"

## Identity Profile (as self-reported)
- Caretaking relationships (ducks, chickens)
- Protective instincts (threatening predators, yard guardianship)
- Messiness / scattershot mindset (non-linear processing)
- Crafting and artistic work (already published)
- Free play with objects
- Reading

## Psychological Observations

### Pre-Exercise Notes
- Self-articulation of identity at age 9 is notably mature
- Identity organized around *relationships* and *actions* rather than static traits
- "Scattershot" self-awareness suggests metacognition about own thinking style

### During-Exercise Observations
1. **Scale ambition without constraint**: Initial wish was "giant waterpark" - she aimed BIG without self-limiting to "realistic"
2. **Territorial grounding**: "in my yard" - anchored the fantastical to her domain, consistent with protective/territorial identity
3. **Relationship over thrill**: When asked what the BEST part of a waterpark was, she chose floating/swimming with ducks - NOT slides, speed, or adrenaline
4. **Immersion desire**: "with ducks surrounding me" - she wants to be IN their world, at the CENTER of the relationship, not observing from outside
5. **Sensory + relational fusion**: The wish combines physical sensation (floating) with relationship (ducks surrounding her)

### Key Insight
Her "waterpark" wish was actually a **relationship wish in disguise**. She doesn't want a waterpark - she wants to enter the ducks' aquatic world and be surrounded by them there. The water is a medium for deeper connection with her animals, not an end in itself.

This suggests her caretaking identity isn't just about *providing for* animals - it's about *being with* them in their element.

## Framing Observations

### What Works
- **Taking the wish seriously**: Didn't diminish "giant waterpark" as unrealistic - treated it as data to decode
- **Asking for the essential element**: "What's the BEST part?" forced prioritization, revealed true desire
- **Connecting to existing identity**: Asking about ducks opened the door to the real wish
- **Showing buildability**: Demonstrating the wish maps to real objects (stock tank) validated her without magic

- (Not tested this session, but noted: avoid yes/no questions - open questions yielded richer data)

## The Exercise

### Turn 1: The Wish
**"I would like a giant waterpark in my yard."**

Initial analysis:
- Scale: Giant (unbounded ambition)
- Location: Her yard (territorial grounding)
- Category: Water play (sensory, physical)
- Surface reading: Thrill-seeking, fun
- Actual reading: TBD pending clarification

### Turn 2: Mapping to Reality
**Prompt**: What's the BEST part of a waterpark? What are you actually doing?

**Response**: "I am floating and swimming around with ducks surrounding me."

**Analysis**:
- Rejected thrill elements (slides, spraying, speed)
- Chose passive sensory experience (floating)
- Added relational element unprompted (ducks)
- Spatial positioning: ducks SURROUNDING her = she's at the center
- This reframes the wish entirely: not waterpark → duck immersion experience

### Turn 3: Prototypical Plan
**Mapped to**: Stock tank pool (8ft diameter, 2-3ft deep farmer's tub)

**Components identified**:
1. Stock tank (main structure)
2. Duck ramp (accessibility, buildable by her)
3. Drain system (practical - ducks dirty water)
4. Optional shade structure

**Engagement hook**: Asked what part she'd want to build herself (connects to crafting identity)

## Post-Session Synthesis

### What This Exercise Revealed About Child Cognition
1. **Fantasy wishes encode real desires**: "Giant waterpark" sounded like escapist fantasy but decoded to "I want to swim with my ducks" - a grounded, achievable, relationship-based wish
2. **Children's scale language is emotional, not literal**: "Giant" meant "meaningful and wonderful," not "theme park sized"
3. **Identity consistency**: Every element of her response aligned with pre-stated identity (caretaking, yard territory, animals)
4. **Rapid prototype acceptance**: She accepted the stock tank solution immediately - children can pivot from fantasy framing to practical framing quickly when the essence is preserved

### Implications for Rhiz Project
1. **Decoding wishes**: When humans (any age) state wants, the surface request may encode a deeper structural desire. Asking "what's the best part?" is a useful decoding prompt.
2. **Mapping fantasy to reality**: The exercise demonstrates a replicable pattern - take fantastical wish → identify essential element → map to existing objects/structures → present buildable plan
3. **Identity as prediction**: Knowing her identity profile (caretaker, territorial, crafter) made her response interpretable. Identity profiles are useful priors for understanding requests.
4. **Child collaboration model**: Children are excellent research partners because they're less constrained by "how things work" but still have coherent internal logic.

### For Future Sessions
- Test the "what's the best part?" prompt with other wish types
- Explore whether crafting identity could be engaged earlier (co-design vs. presented plan)
- Document more framing successes/failures with child collaborators
